# Problem

[[efforts/7-document-downloads-and-contact-details/spec]] moved the document that carries Saud's email and phone number out of the published site and into the reader's own browser, and [[efforts/11-private-download-form/spec]] put it behind an address only he uses. The mechanism is a print: the page fills its contact line, calls `window.print()`, and the reader saves the result. That is the document Saud actually sends with an application, and it is the one document nothing in this repository looks at.

- **It comes out with the browser's furniture on it.** The file Saud generated on 2026-09-11 carries, at the foot of both pages, `Resume - Saud Alnasser | https://saud-alnasser.github.io/saud-alnasser/en/resume/#me | 1 of 2 | 11/09/26, 3:33 AM`. None of that is in the document. It is Chrome's print header and footer, drawn in the paper margin that `@page resume { margin: 10mm }` leaves room for. The published `resume.en.pdf` has never shown it, because the render step takes its PDF with the furniture switched off, so the two documents differ by the renderer while the repository believes they are the same document. Criterion 7 of [[efforts/7-document-downloads-and-contact-details/spec]] and criterion 11 of [[efforts/5-sections-and-resume/spec]] both say the document carries no positioned header or footer, and both stayed true the whole time: the furniture is the browser's, not the document's, so nothing that was being checked was wrong and the document a recruiter opens still had an address and a timestamp stamped across it.
- **It comes out two pages long.** The same file is 612 by 792 points, US Letter, and runs to two. That is the budget [[efforts/5-sections-and-resume/spec]] settled on, at requirement 10, on 2026-09-10: the English resume fits A4 and clears Letter by less than a line, the page count depends on which faces the rendering machine resolves the system font stack to, and Saud took the wider budget over bundling a print face. The budget was a fact about the renderer, and the document it produces for an employer is a two-page short resume, which is the one thing a short resume may not be.
- **It comes out under a different name.** The published download saves as `resume.en.pdf`, from the address it is fetched at. The generated one saves as `Resume - Saud Alnasser.pdf`, because Chrome names a printed PDF after the page's `<title>`. One document, produced two ways, arrives on disk as two files with nothing to say they are the same thing.
- **And the pair does not look like anything.** The white and blue band template was chosen in [[efforts/3-site-redesign/spec]] for a parser and it reads like it: a grey band across every heading, a name in flat capitals, and no hierarchy between the document's strongest line and its weakest. It is a record. Saud asked on 2026-09-11 for the resume to be worth looking at, and for the division of labour to be plain: the CV is the whole record, the resume is the document an application takes.

# Goal

The resume Saud generates through the form is one page, carries nothing but the document, and lands on disk under the name the published file already uses. Both documents are redesigned as one pair, still one column of real text that a parser reads exactly as it reads them today. What the resume gives up to reach one page, the CV keeps, because that is the division between the two. Every guarantee the four prior efforts made still holds: one content source, two languages, two themes, no contact detail in any published file, the form behind its marked address, and the quality gates.

# Scope

- The resume's page budget, in the render step and in the dist check, and everywhere the repository states it in prose.
- The page box both documents print in, and what the generated document is therefore free of.
- The name a generated document is saved under.
- The look of both documents: the header, the section headings, the entry rows, and the rhythm between them.
- What the resume carries, where one page will not hold what two held.
- The line on each document page that says which document it is.
- The tests, the render step, and the dist checks that measure all of the above.
- The part of [[efforts/5-sections-and-resume/spec]] that this reverses, marked where a later reader will hit it.

# Requirements

1. **The generated resume carries nothing the document did not put there.** No document title, no address, no page number, no timestamp. What a reader opens is the resume and the white space around it, the same bytes of content the published PDF carries, plus the contact line the form filled in.
2. **The resume is one page.** In English and in Arabic, on A4 and on Letter, published and generated, as measured on the machine that renders what ships. The render step and the dist check refuse a second page and say which language, which paper, and which copy ran over. The CV keeps no budget, as it never had one.
3. **Everything the resume drops is still on the CV.** The two documents are one record and one application, and this effort only moves the line between them. No fact leaves the content source, no entry loses its Arabic, and nothing the site shows today stops being shown.
4. **Both documents are redesigned as one pair, and the resume's share of it returns height rather than spending it.** One column of real text in reading order, standard headings, no table, no image, no positioned element, no keyword that reaches a parser only as decoration. What changes is typographic: the name and the label, the treatment of a section heading, how a period sits against the title it belongs to, and the rhythm between entries. The CV takes the same treatment so the two still read as one person's documents.
5. **A generated document is saved under the name the published one has.** `resume.en.pdf`, `resume.ar.pdf`, `cv.en.pdf`, `cv.ar.pdf`, whichever page produced it, with nothing else changed about the page a reader is looking at once the print is over.
6. **Each document page says what its document is for.** One line, in both languages, on the page and not in the document: the CV is the whole record, the resume is the short one to send with an application. It is chrome, so it prints on neither.
7. **Nothing still says the resume may run to two pages.** Not the render step, not the dist check, not `docs/development.md`, not a comment in the stylesheet or the document component, and not [[efforts/5-sections-and-resume/spec]], whose requirement 10, constraint on the resume's size, assumption about headroom, and risk about the page budget were written the day the budget widened and are what this reverses.

# Acceptance Criteria

1. A render of each resume page taken the way a print dialog takes it, with the header and footer switched on, carries no occurrence of the page title, the page address, a page number, or a date stamp in its extracted text, in both languages. The same render of a CV page still carries them, which is what shows the check is capable of failing.
2. `pnpm render:pdf` writes `resume.en.pdf` and `resume.ar.pdf` at one page on A4 and one page at Letter, writes the filled copies under `.artifacts/` at one page on both papers, and fails naming the locale, the paper, and the copy when any of them runs to two. `pnpm check:dist` does the same over `dist/`, and its success line says how many pages the file has. **Both pass on the CI runner**, which is the only machine whose answer counts, and the integration workflow is green on the pushed branch.
3. Every project, course, certification, skill, and line of text the resume no longer shows is present on the CV page, in `cv.en.pdf` and `cv.ar.pdf`, and in both `resume.json` documents. `pnpm build` prints `[localized] 0 gaps`, `pnpm test:content` passes, and no file under `src/content/` loses an entry.
4. Both document pages have no `<table>`, no `<img>`, and no fixed or absolute positioned element carrying content. The extraction check finds, in reading order, the name, every experience entry's position, period, and organisation, and every education entry's degree, period, and institution, over `resume.en.pdf`, `cv.en.pdf`, and the filled copy of each, and fails on a missing line. The contrast tests pass on every colour the redesign introduces, in both themes, and Lighthouse still reports at least 90 on the three categories for the home, CV, and resume pages in both languages on the mobile profile.
5. Generating a document through the form saves it as `resume.<locale>.pdf` or `cv.<locale>.pdf`. A test asserts the page's title is that name at the moment the print begins and is back to the page's own title once it is over, in both languages, and that a reader who dismisses the dialog instead of generating never sees the title change.
6. Each document page shows one line naming what that document is, in both languages, and neither line appears in any of the four published PDFs or in a generated one.
7. A search across `scripts/`, `src/`, `docs/`, and `.aep/` for the two-page budget returns only the sentences that record it as history. `.aep/efforts/5-sections-and-resume/spec.md` requirement 10 says one page and points here for why it changed back.

# Constraints

- **No bundled print typeface.** Put to Saud on 2026-09-11 with the alternative, and he chose to reach one page by cutting content instead, as he chose the wider budget over the same face on 2026-09-10. The consequence is unchanged and has to be stated rather than rediscovered: the system font stack means the page count is a property of the machine that renders, a developer's machine will keep saying one page before the runner does, and the render step in CI is the only measurement that decides anything.
- **The type size floor is 10pt, and this effort does not move it.** Fixed by [[efforts/5-sections-and-resume/spec]] and restated by [[efforts/7-document-downloads-and-contact-details/spec]]: a parser and a reader both need the text at the size it reads at, so a document that will not fit is fixed in its content, never in its type size.
- **Nothing about the look is bought with parser safety.** The documents are hiring documents before they are designed objects. A sidebar, a two-column body, an icon standing in for a word, a skill shown as a bar, and a heading that reaches a parser as an image are each refused, whatever they would look like.
- **The white space a generated resume prints with is the document's own.** Removing the page box's margin is what removes the browser's furniture ([[efforts/13-one-page-resume-and-clean-generation/evidence/prototypes/print-furniture-and-the-page-box]]), so the margin has to come back as part of the document, and the page has to stay inside the border a desktop printer cannot reach, which is around 6mm at its worst edge.
- **The form, the marker, and the contact rule are untouched.** The site still publishes no email address and no phone number in any file, the form still stores nothing and sends nothing, it still opens only at the marked address, and the no-script guarantee still holds on both document pages.
- **A shortened text is still true.** Cutting is allowed as far as one page needs and no further, and what is left says what was true before it was cut. A shortened text is the content source's, so the site, the CV, and both JSON documents show it too.
- Everything the four prior efforts constrain still binds: free static hosting, one content source, two languages with one set of facts, truthful academic status, no tracking, no external request, progressive script, stacked changes through Graphite.

# Out of Scope

- **The generated CV's print furniture.** It keeps the header and the footer, and the reason is the mechanism rather than an oversight: the furniture is drawn in the paper margin, the margin is what a document breaking across pages needs on every page, and CSS padding applies at the ends of a box rather than at every fragment of it. The resume can give its margin up because it is one page; the CV runs to five. Reopening this means a second renderer or a library in the reader's browser, which the next entry refuses.
- **A PDF library or a second renderer in the browser.** The generated document is a print of the page the reader is looking at, which is why it needs no dependency, no build step, and no service. Nothing here adds one.
- **A sidebar or a two-column resume.** Put to Saud on 2026-09-11 as the option that looks the most striking, and declined with the reason: it is the layout resume parsers most often scramble, and it would cost criterion 11 of [[efforts/5-sections-and-resume/spec]] on the document he sends with applications.
- **New content.** No project, job, course, or credential is added, and none is removed from the content source. What changes is which of them the resume shows.
- **The order of any section**, in either document, which [[efforts/7-document-downloads-and-contact-details/spec]] put to Saud with the research and settled.
- **A JSON Resume document for the resume.** The machine-readable output follows the CV, as [[efforts/5-sections-and-resume/spec]] fixed.
- **The site's own pages.** The home, work, and education pages, the cards, the certificate dialog, the theme control, and the language menu are not touched. The redesign here reaches the two document pages and the print stylesheet.
- **Reviewing the Arabic.** New Arabic strings are drafts until Saud reads them on the published site.

# Assumptions

- **Chrome names a printed PDF after the page's title, with `.pdf` appended.** Not inferred: the file Saud supplied is called `Resume - Saud Alnasser.pdf` and the resume page's title is `Resume - Saud Alnasser`. So the name is reachable by setting the title for the duration of the print and putting it back afterwards, and the same lever is the one that can get it wrong for a reader who never generates anything.
- **The zero page box is what the reader's dialog starts from.** Measured for the renderer ([[efforts/13-one-page-resume-and-clean-generation/evidence/prototypes/print-furniture-and-the-page-box]]); a print dialog also lets a reader choose a margin of their own, and one who does gets the furniture back. The document is right by default, which is what this effort can guarantee.
- **One page is reachable by dropping one project and by what the redesign returns.** [[efforts/7-document-downloads-and-contact-details/spec]] already recorded which one: "If the page budget refuses three, cachescribe is the one to drop", because the CV keeps it whole and it is the oldest of the three. The measurement that decides is the render step on the runner, and the levers past that one, in the order the earlier effort put them, are the project summaries and the placement's bullets.
- The contact line the form fills stays one line at one page. It is two items longer on a generated document than on a published one, and the fit has to hold for the copy that carries them, which is the copy `.artifacts/` keeps for exactly this reason.

# Risks

- **One page has no headroom, and the runner is the only judge.** This is the failure that stopped the branch on 2026-09-10: the work measures one page on Windows and two on the Linux runner, and the deploy runs the same render step, so a resume that goes over does not fail quietly, it stops the site updating. The guard is the render step and it fires in CI. The remedy that would end it for good is the bundled face, now declined twice, and the fallback is another content lever from the list above.
- **Marking one more entry for the resume will fail the build.** That was already true at two pages and is more true at one. It is a single field on an entry, it is the field Saud is most likely to reach for, and what it costs is a red integration run rather than a wrong document.
- **Cutting a project makes the resume weaker in the way that matters.** cachescribe is a package published to a public registry, which is a distinct signal from the other two. It stays on the CV, on the work page, and in both JSON documents, and the resume is the document where a recruiter spends six seconds, so the trade is made on that reader's behalf rather than on the record's.
- **A redesign can cost height instead of returning it.** More air between entries is exactly what makes a document look considered, and it is the thing this effort has none of. The page count in CI is what catches it, and the order of work has to put the fit after the look rather than before it.
- **The title lever can leak.** A page whose title is left as `resume.en` after a dismissed dialog, a cancelled print, or a browser that fires no `afterprint` is a page whose tab, history entry, and bookmark say `resume.en`. The same three-way clear the contact line already has is what this needs, and a test that only ever generates will not see it.
