# Question

For a 2026 Saudi computer science graduate applying to software developer roles in Saudi Arabia, what does a resume have to contain and in what order, and which personal fields do Saudi employers and their application systems require that a Western-convention resume omits?

All sources were read on **2026-09-10**. Web search was used only to locate pages; SEO articles from resume-optimisation vendors that the searches surfaced (atsverification.com, tailormycv.app, labeeb.ae, resumegyani.in, applyarc.com, boostmyresume.org and similar) are cited nowhere in this file.

# Findings

## Section order

**F1. Bayt.com's own CV template puts professional experience before education, and adds a personal details block at the end.** (source, S15) The template's section order is: name and a single contact line (phone, email, site), an unlabelled summary paragraph, `PROFESSIONAL EXPERIENCE`, `EDUCATION`, `SKILLS`, `PERSONAL DETAILS`. The personal details block contains exactly three prompts: "Add address here", "Add nationality here", "Add date of birth here (DD/MM/YYYY)". True of the template Bayt published on 2024-04-03 for the MENA market generally, not of Saudi Arabia specifically, and not tied to any role level.

**F2. That same template tells a candidate with no professional experience to fill the experience section with internships rather than to move education up.** (source, S15) "if you don't have any previous professional experience, you can include internships instead. For each internship, highlight a key project you worked on, your role in it, and the outcome, focusing on what you learned and the skills you developed." (interpretation) On Bayt's model a CS499 cooperative-training placement would occupy the experience section, and the experience-first order would stand. This is one template from one platform, not a market rule.

**F3. The one Saudi university guide I could read lists education before experience.** (source, S17) UPM's page enumerates the sections as contact information, education, experience, then optional sections (skills, volunteering, campus involvement, leadership, honours, study abroad). (interpretation) A guide listing sections in an order is weaker evidence than a template laying them out, because the list may be pedagogical rather than prescriptive. The page carries no statement that education must precede experience.

**F4. The two artifacts that imply an order disagree, and neither is Saudi-specific for a fresh graduate.** (conclusion) Bayt (S15) is regional and experience-first; UPM (S17) is a Saudi institution but its text is generic Western career-centre material, education-first, and it contains nothing about nationality, date of birth, or the local market. Nothing I found settles the order for exactly this shape, a 2026 Saudi CS graduate whose only experience entry is a 2026 cooperative-training placement.

**F5. What I looked for and did not find.** No Saudi government body, no Saudi university career centre reachable from here, and no Saudi employer publishes a prescribed section order for a fresh-graduate resume. Jadarat, the national employment platform, publishes no CV structure at all (see F6 and F22). Searches in Arabic for a university career-centre CV guide returned only commercial CV-builder marketing.

## Personal fields

**F6. Jadarat does not ask a Saudi job seeker to type personal data; it takes identity from the national single sign-on and populates the rest from government registers.** (source, S1, S2, S3) HRDF states the applicant "must be a Saudi national", that login is via the National Unified Access portal with "national ID number and password", and that "Some of your personal information will be automatically added from various sources, such as NAFATH, the electronic services of the Human Resources Development Fund, Jadara, Saudi Post, and others". The Jadarat manual (S3, page 2) shows the same: the user enters the national ID number and the Nafath password. (interpretation) On the national platform, nationality, date of birth, gender and national ID are facts the state already holds; they are not resume content, and a Saudi applicant never types them. (observation) I could not reach jadarat.sa itself from this environment (connection failure), so the profile form's own field list is unverified.

**F7. Jadarat accepts a CV as a PDF attachment of at most 2 MB.** (source, S2) The page names, as things added to the professional file: experience, professional certificates, professional licences, other certificates, and "CV (as a PDF attachment, maximum 2 MB)". True of Jadarat as HRDF described it on 2026-09-10.

**F8. Bayt.com's CV builder demands the full Gulf personal-details set before anything else.** (source, S16) Bayt's own description of its builder: the first thing a new registrant is asked for is personal information, "requiring you to fill in your full name as it appears in your passport, your date of birth, assigned gender, in addition to your primary nationality as well as any additional nationalities you may hold. You will also need to provide your current country of residence, visa status, marital status, number of dependents, and where your driving license was issued from." True of the Bayt profile builder as documented between 2017 and its April 2022 update; I could not create an account to observe the live form, so the current field list is unverified. Note the gap against Bayt's own template (F1), which asks for only address, nationality and date of birth.

**F9. Bayt treats a profile photograph as a scored component of the profile, not of the CV.** (source, S16) The author reports her profile strength "was stuck on an annoying 98%" until she added a picture, and asserts "more than four in five job seekers have stated they have a picture on their CV". (interpretation) The first is an observation about Bayt's profile-strength meter and is reasonably reliable as a statement about the product. The second is an undated Bayt survey claim with no methodology given in the article; it is marketing copy and should be treated as weak. Bayt's own CV template (S15) has no photograph.

**F10. Saudi employers demand the national ID as a document at the offer or programme-acceptance stage, not as a line on the resume.** (source, S12) Petro Rabigh's Tamheer page lists required documents as "A copy of the Saudi national ID." and "Graduation Certificate showing the cumulative GPA from the university or college." (interpretation) These are attachments demanded of a candidate already in a process. Nothing on that page, or on any employer page I read, asks for a national ID number inside a CV.

**F11. No Saudi employer portal I could observe asks the applicant for a photograph, national ID number, Iqama number, marital status or date of birth at the point of applying.** (observation) stc (S8, S9, S10) and Elm (S11) run SAP SuccessFactors career sites; Elm's FAQ says only "When you create your profile on the Elm Careers site you'll be asked to share your career history by uploading an existing resume or importing your information from LinkedIn." Neither site's public pages enumerate application fields, and both put the application form behind registration, which I did not complete. This is an absence of evidence about the forms, not evidence that the fields are absent.

**F12. SAP SuccessFactors does ship date of birth and gender as standard candidate profile fields, so whether a Saudi employer asks for them is that employer's configuration.** (source, S13, "Candidate Profile Field Definition: Field ID and Field Type", 1H 2026) The standard field IDs listed include `contactEmail`, `primaryEmail`, `firstName`, `lastName`, `middleName`, `gender` (type "derived"), `ethnicity`, `race`, `address`, `dateOfAvail`, `cellPhone`, `homePhone`, `faxNumber`, `city`, `state`, `country`, `zip`, and `dateOfBirth` (type "date"). (observation) No `nationality`, `nationalId` or `iqama` field appeared in the snippets I could read; the snippet was truncated, so their absence from the standard list is not established. (conclusion) The date-of-birth field exists in the product that stc and Elm run. Whether their templates enable it cannot be determined from outside.

**F13. Saudi regulation now prohibits the employer from discriminating on several of exactly these attributes, and says nothing about what an applicant may put on a CV.** (source, S5, general controls, item 1) "يجب ألا يحتوي الإعلان عن الشواغر الوظيفية وإجراء المقابلات الوظيفية على أي نوع من أنواع التمييز كالتمييز على أساس الجنس، أو الإعاقة، أو السن، أو الحالة الاجتماعية أو أي شكل من أشكال التمييز الأخرى." My translation: the advertisement of job vacancies and the conduct of job interviews must not contain any kind of discrimination, such as discrimination on the basis of gender, disability, age, marital status, or any other form of discrimination. The interview controls, item 5.a, prohibit the panel from "طرح الأسئلة المتعلقة بالحريات الشخصية وما شابه ذلك أثناء إجراء المقابلة الوظيفية", that is, asking questions relating to personal liberties and the like during the interview. True of the private sector, from the decision published 23 March 2025.

**F14. Nationality is conspicuously not in that prohibited list.** (observation, S5) Gender, disability, age and marital status are named; nationality is not. (interpretation) This is consistent with Saudization, under which nationality is a lawful and load-bearing hiring criterion: Jadarat (S1), Tamheer (S4), stc COOP and TIP (S8, S9) all state "Saudi national" as an eligibility condition. (conclusion) Of the fields in the question, nationality is the one the market's own gates actually turn on, and it is the one Bayt's template asks for by name (F1). Age and marital status are the ones a 2025 regulation now restricts the employer from acting on.

**F15. Saudi-format phone number: unsourced.** (observation) I looked for a portal that requires or validates a +966 number and found none I could observe. Jadarat verifies a mobile number (S1: "verify your email and mobile number") but does not publish a format rule. Secondary CV-advice sites assert a +966 convention; none of them is a primary source and none is cited here. Treat the +966 claim as unsourced.

## What earns its place with no full-time employment history

**F16. GPA is a hard gate at the largest Saudi employers, published as a number, and it differs by employer.** (source, S7, S8, S9, S12)
  - Aramco, graduate applicants (defined as "less than three years of work experience", requiring a "Bachelor degree or higher obtained through full-time study"): for technical majors, which the page lists as including computer science and IT, "Minimum GPA ... is 2.0 out of 4 (or 3.0 out of 5 or equivalent)". For administrative majors, 2.5 out of 4.
  - stc, Cooperative Training Program: "Academic excellence with a minimum of 3.2 out of 4.0, and 4.0 out of 5.0 GPA, or equivalent."
  - stc, Talent Incubation Program, which targets "Saudi fresh graduates with less than a years of experience": the same 3.2 out of 4.0 / 4.0 out of 5.0 floor.
  - Petro Rabigh, Tamheer: "The applicant's GPA must not be less than 2.5 out of 4 or 3.5 out of 5", plus a graduation certificate showing the cumulative GPA.
(conclusion) For a fresh graduate in this market the GPA is a screening number that several major employers publish in advance, and one of them requires documentary proof of it.

**F17. The national graduate-training programme itself sets no GPA condition; individual employers add one.** (source, S4) HRDF's Tamheer conditions are: Saudi national; registered on the HRDF website; a diploma of at least one academic year, or a bachelor's degree or higher from a Saudi or accredited foreign university; not over 30 years of age; no more than one year of prior practical experience; not currently employed; not registered with GOSI; not on the Fund's prohibited list; not having benefited from Tamheer more than twice. No GPA appears. (interpretation) The 2.5/4 floor on the Petro Rabigh Tamheer page (S12) is that employer's own filter layered on top of the programme.

**F18. English proficiency is screened numerically for fresh graduates at stc, alongside GPA.** (source, S9) The Talent Incubation Program requires "English Language Proficiency with a minimum scores: IELTS: 5 or equivalent, TOFEL: 35 or equivalent, STEP: 85 or equivalent". The Cooperative Training Program lists "English Language Proficiency" without a score (S8).

**F19. A cooperative-training placement is a named, institutionally recognised category at Saudi employers, not an improvised entry.** (source, S8) stc runs a "Cooperative Training Program" as a standing track with its own page and job category, describing it as helping trainees in "meeting the graduation requirements of their respective universities", targeting "Under graduate students who required to complete the Coop training". stc's careers navigation separates "Trainee", "Fresh graduate" and "Experienced" as distinct candidate tracks. (interpretation) The market has a name and a slot for exactly the CS499 placement.

**F20. On projects: one source, and it attaches them to education rather than giving them a section.** (source, S15) Bayt's annotated template, in the note beside the `EDUCATION` block: "If you have a higher education degree, there's no need to list high school. Include any extracurricular activities/training next to your degree. For those in STEM fields, mentioning relevant projects or theses can add value." Its skills note adds: "For technical roles, emphasize software proficiency or technical skills upfront." UPM (S17) says "You might also list relevant courses and projects you have completed", also under education. (conclusion) Both sources that mention projects treat them as material attached to the degree, not as a standalone section. Neither addresses open-source or personal projects, and neither addresses a team senior project specifically.

**F21. On certificates: they are collected as first-class objects by the national platform and by Bayt, and the one template that mentions them folds them into education.** (source, S2) Jadarat's professional file has slots for "professional certificates", "professional licenses" and "other certificates" as separate items from the CV attachment. (source, S16) Bayt's builder has a "training and certifications" section. (source, S15) Bayt's template instead puts them under education, with the instruction "List certifications that are current and directly relevant to the job. For example, 'AWS Certified Solutions Architect (2022)'." (interpretation) The example given is a paid industry certification, not an online course completion; no source I read distinguishes online course certificates from professional certifications, or says whether the former are worth listing.

**F22. What I looked for and did not find.** No source I read ranks a graduation project, a GPA, personal or open-source projects, and online course certificates against each other, for this market or any other. No Saudi source at all discusses open-source contributions or a personal project portfolio. The ranking asked for in the brief is not established by anything I found.

## Length and language

**F23. The two sources that state a length both say one page, and neither is Saudi-market evidence about fresh graduates.** (source, S17) UPM: "Your resumes should only be one page in length. A good rule to follow is one page for every 8-10 years of experience." (observation, S15) Bayt's shipped CV template is a single page. (interpretation) UPM's rule is transplanted Western guidance; Bayt's template is a design decision, not a stated rule. Neither is a Saudi employer or portal saying what it expects. Jadarat's only stated constraint is a file size, 2 MB (F7), not a page count.

**F24. Everything a Saudi software employer put in front of me was in English, on career sites that also offer Arabic.** (observation, S8, S9, S10, S11) stc's careers site serves English and العربية; Elm's serves العَرَبِيَّة (السعودية), English (United Kingdom) and English (United States). Both sites' programme pages, eligibility criteria and job categories are written in English. Aramco's graduate criteria (S7) are in English. Conversely, Jadarat is Arabic-first with an "Eng" toggle, and HRDF publishes its Jadarat and Tamheer user manuals only in Arabic (S3, and the PDF listing on the HRDF site, where the Jadarat manual exists in Arabic only).

**F25. Saudi labour law makes Arabic the language of the employment relationship, but that duty falls on the employer and starts after hiring.** (source, S6, Article 9) "Arabic shall be the language used for data, records, files, employment contracts, and the like as provided for in this Law or in any decision issued in implementation of its provisions as well as the instructions issued by the employer to his workers. If the employer uses a foreign language besides Arabic in any of the aforementioned cases, the Arabic text shall prevail." (interpretation) A job applicant is not yet a worker and a CV is not among the enumerated documents. This article does not require an Arabic CV. It is recorded here because it is the provision most likely to be misread as requiring one.

**F26. There is one hard technical argument against an Arabic-only CV, and it is in F28.**

**F27. What I looked for and did not find.** No Saudi employer, portal or government body states a preferred or required CV language for software roles. No source states a page count expected of a fresh graduate in Saudi Arabia. Whether a bilingual or dual-language CV is normal in Riyadh is unsourced.

## Parsing

**F28. SAP SuccessFactors does not list Arabic among the languages its resume parser supports.** (source, S13, "Configuring Resume Parsing", version 1H 2026, dated 2026-08-14) "Resume parsing is only supported in the following languages: Dutch, English, German, French, Spanish, Swedish, Danish, Polish, Romanian, Italian, Slovak, Czech, Russian, Portuguese, and Chinese". (observation) stc and Elm both run SuccessFactors (F30). (conclusion) At those two employers, an Arabic CV falls outside the documented parsing coverage; an English one does not. This is the single most consequential parsing fact I found for this market, and it is the vendor's own statement.

**F29. SAP names its parser vendor and disclaims accuracy.** (source, S13) "SAP SucessFactors uses the third-party software Textkernel to parse resume data." (the misspelling is SAP's). "Resume parsing isn't always 100% accurate. This is a known limitation." "Resume parsing requires a third party to know exactly where data is placed." "The creation of candidates through API does not support parsing." Also, of the recruiter-side batch upload: "The system can't parse resumes correctly when they're missing country/region information." (interpretation) The last of these is a concrete, actionable rule beyond the one-column, real-text, no-table rules of S18: a country or region must be present in the document. It is stated in the batch-upload topic, so whether it applies to a candidate's own upload is not established.

**F30. stc and Elm run SAP SuccessFactors Recruiting; Aramco and SABIC could not be checked.** (observation, S10, S11) Both careers.stc.com.sa and career.elm.sa load assets from rmkcdn.successfactors.com, and stc's cookie disclosure names "SAP as service provider" with the SuccessFactors career-site cookies `careerSiteCompanyId`, `JSESSIONID` and `route`. (observation, S12) Petro Rabigh's career site runs Teamtailor, not an enterprise ATS. (observation) careers.aramco.com and recruitment.sabic.com refused every connection from this environment; Aramco's and SABIC's application systems are therefore unidentified here.

**F31. SuccessFactors' candidate-facing upload format list omits PDF, while its recruiter-side list includes it.** (source, S13) "Working with Resume Parsing" (1H 2026): "You can upload resume with DOCX, PPT, and TXT format. You can also upload TIFF, JPG, BMP, and GIF image types for parsing resumes." "Uploading Multiple Candidate Resumes in Batches" (1H 2026): "Resume are no larger than 5 MB and are in one of the following formats: DOCX, PDF, TXT, TIFF, JPG, BMP, GIF", up to 100 at a time. (interpretation) I read both statements as search snippets rather than full pages, and PDF's absence from the candidate list may be an omission in SAP's own text rather than a product restriction. Do not treat "SuccessFactors rejects a candidate's PDF" as established. What is established is that the recruiter-side pipeline accepts PDF at up to 5 MB, and that image formats are accepted for parsing on both paths, which implies OCR somewhere in the chain.

**F32. What I looked for and did not find.** Nothing published by any Saudi employer, by Jadarat, or by any Saudi-hosted ATS describes how it parses a resume. No Saudi-specific parsing guidance exists that I could locate. There is no Arabic-language equivalent of the vendor parsing documentation. SAP KBA 2081576, whose title promises the parsing feature "and its limitations", is behind an SAP login and was not read.

# Conclusion

Taking the five parts of the question in order, and reporting only what the sources carry.

1. **Section order.** Not settled, and the two artifacts that imply an order disagree. Bayt.com, the region's dominant job platform, ships a template that leads with professional experience and tells a candidate with no employment to fill that section with internships (F1, F2). The one Saudi university guide reachable from here lists education first, but its text is generic Western career-centre material with nothing Saudi in it (F3). For the exact shape in question, a 2026 CS graduate whose sole experience entry is a 2026 cooperative-training placement, no source speaks (F4). What is established is that the market has a named category for that placement and treats trainees, fresh graduates and experienced hires as separate tracks (F19).

2. **Personal fields.** The fields split three ways.
   - *Nationality* is the one field the market's gates actually turn on. Every Saudi programme and employer track I read states "Saudi national" as an eligibility condition (F14), and Bayt's own template asks for it by name (F1). It is also the one attribute absent from the 2025 anti-discrimination list (F14).
   - *Date of birth* is asked for by Bayt's template and builder (F1, F8) and exists as a standard field in the ATS stc and Elm run (F12), but no Saudi employer portal was observed asking for it. Age is now a prohibited basis for discrimination in ads and interviews (F13).
   - *Marital status, dependents, visa status and gender* are asked for by Bayt's builder (F8), are absent from Bayt's own template (F1), and are, for gender and marital status, prohibited bases for discrimination under the 2025 controls (F13).
   - *A photograph* is scored by Bayt's profile meter but absent from Bayt's CV template, and no employer portal was observed asking for one (F9, F11).
   - *National ID or Iqama number* is demanded as a document copy at the programme-acceptance stage (F10) and as a login credential on the national platform (F6). No source asks for it inside a CV.
   - *A +966 phone format* is unsourced (F15).
   The deeper structural fact: on the national platform a Saudi applicant types none of this, because Nafath supplies it (F6). "Required by the system" and "belongs on the resume" are different questions here, and only the first is answered.

3. **What earns its place.** GPA is established: Aramco publishes 2.0/4 for computer science, stc publishes 3.2/4 for both its coop and its fresh-graduate programmes, Petro Rabigh publishes 2.5/4 for Tamheer and demands a certificate proving it (F16, F17). English proficiency is screened numerically at stc (F18). Projects and certificates are mentioned by only two sources, both of which attach them to the education entry rather than granting them a section, and the certificate example given is a paid industry certification (F20, F21). Nothing found ranks a graduation project, personal projects, open-source work, or online course certificates against each other, in this market or otherwise (F22).

4. **Length and language.** One page is what both sources that address length indicate, but neither is a Saudi employer stating an expectation (F23). Jadarat's only published constraint is a 2 MB PDF (F7). On language: every Saudi software employer's own material I read is in English on a site that also offers Arabic, while the government job platform is Arabic-first (F24). Labour law's Arabic requirement binds the employer after hiring and does not reach a CV (F25). No source states a CV language expectation for software roles in Riyadh (F27). The one hard argument in the file is technical, not conventional, and it is in point 5.

5. **Parsing.** Beyond the general rules already recorded in S18, two Saudi-relevant facts are established from SAP's own 1H 2026 documentation: Arabic is not among the fifteen languages SuccessFactors' parser supports, and stc and Elm both run SuccessFactors (F28, F30); and the system "can't parse resumes correctly when they're missing country/region information", stated of the recruiter-side batch path (F29). SAP names Textkernel as the parser and states plainly that parsing "isn't always 100% accurate" (F29). The candidate-facing format list omits PDF while the recruiter-side list includes it; this is not established as a restriction and should not be treated as one (F31). No Saudi employer or portal documents its own parsing behaviour at all (F32).

# Not checked

- **jadarat.sa itself.** Every connection from this environment failed. The job seeker profile's actual field list, the order of its sections, and the structure of any CV it generates are therefore unverified. HRDF's published Jadarat manuals cover login and application steps only.
- **careers.aramco.com and recruitment.sabic.com.** Both refused all connections. Which ATS Aramco and SABIC run, and what their application forms ask for, are unknown. The claim in circulation that Aramco uses Taleo was not verified and is not recorded as a finding.
- **Any application form behind registration.** I did not create an account on Jadarat, Bayt, stc, Elm, or any Workday, Taleo or SuccessFactors instance. Every statement about employer application fields in this file is from public pages or vendor documentation, never from the form.
- **Bayt.com's live CV builder.** Blocked by the site (HTTP 403) on most fetches. The field list in F8 is from Bayt's own 2017 article last updated April 2022, not from the current form.
- **Bayt.com's Saudi-specific guides.** "How to find a job in Saudi Arabia in 2026" and "CV format for UAE jobs: the complete 2026 guide" were both blocked. A search snippet of the first claims an Iqama number belongs on an expatriate's Saudi CV; that claim is unverified, is about expatriates rather than Saudi nationals, and is not recorded as a finding.
