# Question

For a Saudi software developer applying to software roles in Saudi Arabia in 2026, do professional resumes and the application forms of Saudi employers and hiring platforms carry a languages section, and where they do: what is it called (English and Arabic), which languages does it list, and what proficiency vocabulary or scale does it use (a word ladder, the CEFR bands, a test score such as IELTS, TOEFL or STEP, or something else)?

All sources were read on **2026-09-11** unless a source line says otherwise. This file extends `.aep/efforts/7-document-downloads-and-contact-details/evidence/research/saudi-resume-conventions.md` (S22 below) and does not restate it. Web search was used only to locate pages; SEO articles from CV-builder vendors (lambacv, cvwizard, visualcv, boostmyresume, resumita and similar) that the searches surfaced are cited nowhere in this file. The one Saudi platform blog cited (Sabbar, S8) is marked secondary throughout.

# Findings

## Saudi hiring platforms

**F1. Jadarat's job-seeker profile carries a languages section, named "اللغات" in Arabic and "Languages" in English, inside the professional file.** (source, S1) `LanguagesData_V2.mvc.js` and `LanguagesData.mvc.js` carry the strings "اللغات", "إضافة لغة", "إضافة لغة جديدة", "تعديل اللغة", "تم إضافة اللغة بنجاح", and their English resources "Languages", "Add new language", "Edit language", "Language has been added successfully". The profile view screen (`NewViewProfileWB.mvc.js`) names the profile's tabs as "الرئيسية | المعلومات الشخصية والتواصل | التعليم | الملف المهني | التفضيلات | التقديم | السيرة الذاتية | ملفاتي" and mounts a container named `LanguagesSection` with `visible: true`. (observation) The section sits under the professional file ("الملف المهني") alongside experience, skills, and certificates and licences, which is the same grouping HRDF describes in S2 and S22 F7. True of the Jadarat build with version sequence 5282 on 2026-09-11.

**F2. Each Jadarat language entry has exactly two required fields, "اللغة" (Language) and "مستوى الإتقان" (Proficiency Level), both dropdowns filled from server lookups, and the platform caps the list at five languages.** (source, S1) `LanguageItem_V2.mvc.js` labels the two dropdowns "اللغة" and "مستوى الإتقان" (the older `LanguageItem.mvc.js` carries the English labels "Language" and "Proficiency Level"), validates both with "هذا الحقل إلزامي" / "This field is required.", and fills them through the data actions `DataActionGetLanguageLookup` and `DataActionGetProficiencyLevelLookup`. `LanguagesData_V2.mvc.js` refuses a sixth entry with "عفوا، لا يمكنك ان تتخطي الحد الاقصى لعدد اللغات : (5)" / "Sorry , you cannot exceed the maximum number of languages: (5)", and a duplicate with "تم اضافة هذه اللغة سابقا" / "This Language already added before". (observation) **The proficiency vocabulary itself is not in the client code.** It is a server-side lookup that requires a Nafath login to call, so the words Jadarat offers under "مستوى الإتقان" (whether a ladder such as ممتاز / جيد, a CEFR band, or something else) are not established by this file. What is established is that the applicant picks from a closed list the platform owns; the applicant does not type a level.

**F3. Jadarat attaches language test certificates to a language entry, and its earlier form hard-coded exactly two tests, IELTS and TOEFL, with their score ranges; STEP is absent from the code.** (source, S1) The older `LanguageItem.mvc.js` renders two radio buttons whose texts are "TOEFL" (value `0000201`) and "IELTS" (value `0000202`), a score input, and validation that fires only when the chosen language is the lookup entry whose English name is "english" (`englishCodeVar`): "Please enter value between 1 and 9" for IELTS and "Please enter value between 1 and 120" for TOEFL. The current `LanguageItem_V2.mvc.js` drops the radio buttons for a free-text "اسم الشهادة" ("Insert Certificate Name"), a "الدرجة" ("Insert Score") input, a certificate file upload ("تحميل ملف الشهادة", "ملف الشهادة"), a "معتمد" (verified) marker, and a cap of two certificates per language ("عفوا، لا يمكنك ادخال أكثر من شهادتين للغة الواحدة"). Which of the two forms a user sees is chosen by the feature toggle `NJ1904_LanguageSectionUpdateInProfessionalSkills`. (observation) The strings "STEP", "CEFR", "A1", "native", "fluent" occur nowhere in any of the eight scripts. (interpretation) On the national platform the test score is a certificate attached to a language, separate from the proficiency level, and it is optional. The IELTS 1 to 9 and TOEFL 0 to 120 ranges are the only score scales the platform ever encoded.

**F4. Jadarat parses an uploaded CV to propose profile entries, and languages is one of the five sections it extracts.** (source, S1) `Profile.AI.Profile.mvc.js` carries the section headings "الخبرات | المهارات | اللغات | الرخص والشهادات | المؤهلات", the messages "تمت إضافة اللغات بنجاح" / "حدث خطأ أثناء إضافة اللغات", the same five-language cap, and "عذرًا، لقد تم تحسين الملف الشخصي بالفعل باستخدام السيرة الذاتية التي تم تحميلها." (my translation: sorry, the profile has already been enhanced using the uploaded CV). (interpretation) A languages section in the PDF a Saudi applicant uploads to Jadarat (S22 F7: PDF, 2 MB) is machine-read into the platform's own languages list. What the parser does with an English-language CV, or with a level written as a word rather than a lookup value, is not established.

**F5. Jadarat's job application step asks nothing about languages.** (source, S1) `SubmitApplication.mvc.js` carries only a confirmation dialogue: "هل أنت متأكد من التقديم على وظيفة", "تقديم", "تم التقديم بنجاح", and refusal messages. (conclusion) On Jadarat the languages question is answered once, in the profile, never per application. This matches S22 F6: the profile is the application.

**F6. Bayt's shipped CV template has no languages section at all.** (source, S3) The full text layer of the one-page template contains the headings `PROFESSIONAL EXPERIENCE`, `EDUCATION`, `SKILLS`, `PERSONAL DETAILS` and the word "language" does not occur anywhere in the template body or its nine annotations. (observation) This settles a question S22 F1 left open by listing the sections without a text search. Bayt's profile builder, which may have its own languages block, returned HTTP 403 to every fetch (see Not checked).

**F7. The one Saudi platform template that shows a languages line writes it as "اللغات: العربية (اللغة الأم)، الإنجليزية (طليق)" in Arabic and "Languages: Arabic: Native, English: Fluent" in English.** (source, S4, "نماذج سيرة ذاتية سعودية احترافية", 2026-02-13) The Arabic sample CV's line is exactly "اللغات: العربية (اللغة الأم)، الإنجليزية (طليق)."; the English sample CV's block is "Languages: Arabic: Native English: Fluent". The article's section list, item 6, is "اللغات (مع التركيز على العربية والإنجليزية)" and says "اذكر اللغات التي تتقنها ومستوى إتقانك لكل منها. اللغة العربية هي اللغة الأم والضرورية للتواصل في السعودية، بينما تعتبر اللغة الإنجليزية مهارة حيوية في العديد من الشركات متعددة الجنسيات أو الشركات السعودية الكبرى". (interpretation) Secondary, and one platform's blog; but it is the only Saudi-authored template found that renders the section, and it lists exactly two languages, Arabic then English, each with a one-word level.

**F8. That same Saudi platform's guidance offers four different proficiency vocabularies side by side and endorses none.** (source, S4, "اللغات في السيرة الذاتية", 2025-04-24) It presents: the CEFR with Arabic glosses, "A1 - مبتدئ", "A2 - مبتدئ متقدم", "B1 - متوسط", "B2 - متوسط متقدم", "C1 - متقدم", "C2 - متقدم جدًا (الطلاقة)"; the ILR 0 to 5 scale; LinkedIn's ladder with Arabic glosses ("Native/Bilingual Proficiency - إجادة أصلية أو ثنائية اللغة", "Full Professional Proficiency - إجادة مهنية كاملة", ...); the English words "Native (متحدث أصلي)، Fluent (طليق)، Proficient (متقدم)، أو Basic (أساسي)"; and "مصطلحات أقل رسمية مثل \"مبتدئ\"، \"متوسط\"، \"جيد\"، أو \"ممتاز\"". Its worked examples attach test scores to a band: "الإنجليزية: مستوى متقدم (C1) | شهادة IELTS بدرجة 7.5" and "English: Advanced (C1) - IELTS Academic (Score: 7.5, Issued: March 2023)". Its separate teacher-CV article says "إذا كنت تجيد لغات أخرى، أدرجها مع مستوى الإتقان (ممتاز – جيد جدًا – جيد)". (interpretation) The ممتاز / جيد جدًا / جيد ladder that the brief asked me to confirm or refute appears here as advice for teachers, and nowhere in a Saudi employer's or government body's own form (F16). Secondary throughout.

## Saudi employers' application systems

**F9. Three Saudi technology employers on modern applicant tracking systems ask for no languages at all on their application forms.** (source, S5, S6, S7)
  - Tamara (Greenhouse), "Data Governance Lead", Riyadh: `First Name*`, `Last Name*`, `Email*`, `Country*`, `Phone*`, `Location (City)*`, resume (`Attach` / `Enter manually`), `Are you a Saudi National ?*`, and one regulated-industry question. "Senior Manager - Credit (Saudi National)", Riyadh: the same plus `Please share a link to your linkedin profile *`, `Why are you interested in joining Tamara? *`, current and expected salary in SAR.
  - Mozn (Workable), "AI Engineer", Riyadh: `First name`, `Last name`, `Email`, `Phone`, `Resume`, `Cover letter`, one relatives-at-Mozn question. "Engineering Manager", Riyadh: adds `Headline`, `Address`, an `Education` group (`School`, `Field of study`, `Degree`, dates), an `Experience` group (`Title`, `Company`, `Industry`, `Summary`, dates), `Summary`, three free-text questions, availability.
  - Lean Technologies (Ashby), "Senior Compliance Manager", Riyadh and "Staff DevOps Engineer", Dubai: `Resume`, `Name`, `Email`, `Current Company`, `LinkedIn Profile`, `Salary Expectations`, `Additional Information`.
(observation) None of the six forms has a language, Arabic, English, or proficiency field; the words "language" and "proficien" occur in these pages only inside job descriptions (Tamara: "proficiency in Excel"). (interpretation) At these employers the resume is the only place a language claim can be made, and nothing on the form asks for one. True of the forms served on 2026-09-11 for those job postings; each employer configures questions per posting, and the software engineering roles among them were not in Saudi Arabia (Tamara: India; Lean: Dubai) or were Mozn's two Riyadh roles.

**F10. SAP SuccessFactors, which stc and Elm run, ships a candidate-profile languages block with one language field and three proficiency fields, all closed lists whose values each customer defines.** (source, S9) "Candidate Profile Element: Background Information" (1H 2026) gives the sample `<background-element id="languages" type-id="9">` with data fields `language` (`<picklist id="language"/>`, label "Language"), `speakingProf` (label "Speaking Proficiency", `field-name="vfld2"`, `required="true"`), `readingProf` ("Reading Proficiency", `vfld3`), and `writingProf` ("Writing Proficiency", `vfld4`), each proficiency bound to `<picklist id="languageSkillLevel"/>`. The Candidate Pipeline metadata sample (2026-08-24) names the same four fields on `CandidateBackground_Languages`, with the proficiencies typed `PicklistOption` on a picklist called `fluency`. "Maintain My Profile" (1H 2026) describes the candidate's view: a "Language Skills card", "Add Language Skills", and "Language: select from dropdown, Speaking Proficiency: select from dropdown, Reading Proficiency: select from dropdown, Writing Proficiency: select from dropdown". "Configuring Resume Parsing" (1H 2026) includes `<bg-element refid="languages" data-field-id="*"/>` in its field-permission sample, so parsed resume data may write to the block. (observation) No SAP page I could reach lists the picklist's values; SAP's picklist documentation says values are loaded per instance from a CSV. (interpretation) At stc and Elm, if the block is enabled, a language is rated three times (speaking, reading, writing) from a dropdown whose wording is stc's or Elm's choice, and a parsed CV may pre-fill it. Whether either employer enables it, and with what words, cannot be observed without an account (see Not checked).

**F11. Oracle Taleo's Career Section has no standard languages block.** (source, S10) The "Application Flow Blocks" table (chapter 5) enumerates: Additional Information, Attachments, Background Check (Address History, Consent, Military Experience, and others), Basic Profile, Candidate Personal Information, Candidate Personal Information - Agency, Certifications, Competencies, Disqualification Questions, Diversity, Education, General Information, Job Sourcing Tracking, OLF Selection, Plain Text Resume, Prescreening Questionnaire, References, Referrer Personal Information, Resume Upload, Screening, Send New Jobs To, Shift Availability, Work Conditions, Work Experience. Every one of the 40-odd occurrences of "language" in the 2025 guide concerns the application's display language or multilingual content; "proficien" and "fluen" do not occur. (interpretation) A Taleo employer can ask about languages only through a custom question or user-defined field. (observation) No Saudi employer in this file was confirmed to run Taleo; S22 records that the claim that Aramco does was not verified, and careers.aramco.com again refused every connection on 2026-09-11. The URL shapes Aramco publishes (`careers.aramco.com/content/Experience-applicant/?locale=en_US`, `jobs.aramco.com/myprofile/`) match the SuccessFactors career-site pattern stc uses (`careers.stc.com.sa/content/TIP/?locale=en_US`), which is suggestive and nothing more.

**F12. Neither Saudi SuccessFactors employer says a word about languages on its public pages.** (source, S8, S22 F11, F18) Elm's FAQ mentions language only as "uploading an existing resume or importing your information from LinkedIn"; stc's TIP page mentions it only as the IELTS 5 / TOEFL 35 / STEP 85 screen already recorded in S22 F18. (interpretation) The one Saudi software employer that states a language requirement states it as a test score with three accepted tests, not as a word or a CEFR band.

## LinkedIn

**F13. LinkedIn's languages section has one optional proficiency field with a fixed five-step ladder.** (source, S11) The Language object has `id`, `name` ("Localizable language name"), and `proficiency` ("Member's proficiency level of the language", not required) with the enum `ELEMENTARY` (Elementary proficiency), `LIMITED_WORKING` (Limited working proficiency), `PROFESSIONAL_WORKING` (Professional working proficiency), `FULL_PROFESSIONAL` (Full professional proficiency), `NATIVE_OR_BILINGUAL` (Native or bilingual proficiency). True of the LinkedIn API reference as last updated 2021-10-11. (observation) LinkedIn's help-centre article on adding languages could not be located (the URL search results offered returned 404, and the help-centre search endpoint returned 400); the API reference is the vendor's own definition and is treated as primary for the ladder. (interpretation) Elm imports from LinkedIn (S22 F11) and Tamara and Lean ask for a LinkedIn URL (F9), so LinkedIn's five words are the vocabulary a Saudi employer is most likely to receive by import. The Arabic renderings LinkedIn shows for these five values were not checked.

## The scales themselves

**F14. The CEFR self-assessment grid is six bands in three groups, described per skill in first-person "I can" statements, and carries no test scores.** (source, S17) Columns A1, A2 (Basic User), B1, B2 (Independent user), C1, C2 (Proficient user); rows Listening and Reading under Understanding, Spoken interaction and Spoken production under Speaking, and Writing. Every cell is an "I can" statement, for example A1 Listening: "I can understand familiar words and very basic phrases concerning myself, my family and immediate concrete surroundings when people speak slowly and clearly." (interpretation) A CEFR band on a resume is a self-assessment unless a certificate is named beside it.

**F15. IELTS reports bands 0 to 9 in half steps, names each whole band, and maps to the CEFR only at its top.** (source, S18) Band 9 Expert, 8 Very good, 7 Good, 6 Competent, 5 Modest, 4 Limited, 3 Extremely limited, 2 Intermittent, 1 Non-user, 0 Did not attempt; "IELTS test results are considered valid for two years after a test has been taken". (source, S19) Band 8.5 and above "recognised as C2", band 8 "borderline", the 6.5 to 7 boundary "a C1 minimum threshold", from "benchmarking exercises conducted in 2009" and studies "in 2009 and 2010"; the lower bands are shown only in a figure I could not read as text. (observation) The band stc's TIP accepts, 5, is "Modest: partial command of the language and copes with overall meaning in most situations".

**F16. STEP's own score bands, and any ETEC mapping of STEP to the CEFR or to IELTS, are unverified, and the Saudi institutions that publish their own equivalences disagree with one another.** (source, S20) ETEC's pages were unreachable. (source, S15) Prince Sattam bin Abdulaziz University publishes, last modified 2025-10-05, the table IELTS 7 = TOEFL iBT 94 to 101 = STEP 100; 6.5 = 79 to 93 = 97 to 99; 6 = 60 to 78 = "69-83" (as printed); 5.5 = 46 to 59 = 82 to 75; 5 = 35 to 45 = 74 to 67; 4.5 = 32 to 34 = 66 to 64; 0 to 4 = 0 to 31 = 63 to 60. (source, S16) Saudi Electronic University accepts "(IELTS-Academic) with a score of at least 5, (STEP) with a score of no less than 76, (TOEFL_IBT) with a score of no less than 45", and STEP results may be up to 3 years old where IELTS and TOEFL may be 2. (source, S22 F18) stc accepts IELTS 5, TOEFL 35, STEP 85. (observation) Three Saudi institutions equate IELTS 5 with STEP 67 to 74, 76, and 85 respectively; the PSAU row for band 6 is internally inconsistent as published. (interpretation) There is no single STEP-to-IELTS table in circulation from a Saudi source I could read, and none of these is ETEC's. Commercial prep sites that the searches surfaced assert that STEP is CEFR-aligned and scored 0 to 100 in six bands; they contradict each other on the cut-offs and none is cited here. (conclusion) A STEP score on a resume is a number out of 100 whose meaning each reader converts with their own table.

# Conclusion

Taking the question's parts in order, and reporting only what the sources carry:

1. **Is there a languages section?** On the national platform, yes, and it is structured: Jadarat's professional file has a "اللغات" / "Languages" block of up to five entries, each with a language and a proficiency level chosen from closed lists, with optional test certificates attached (F1 to F3). Jadarat also machine-reads languages out of the uploaded CV (F4). At the three Saudi technology employers whose live forms were observed, no (F9); at the SuccessFactors employers the product has a block but whether it is switched on is unobservable (F10). Of the two Saudi CV templates read, Bayt's has no languages section (F6) and Sabbar's has a two-line one (F7). No source asks for languages at the application step; where it is asked at all, it is asked in a profile (F5, F10).

2. **What is it called?** "اللغات" in Arabic and "Languages" in English, in every source that has the section (Jadarat, Sabbar, SuccessFactors' "Language Skills", LinkedIn's languages section, JSON Resume's `languages`). The level field is "مستوى الإتقان" on Jadarat and "Proficiency Level" in its English resources (F2); KSU's form uses "اللغات التي تجيدها" with no level (F17).

3. **Which languages?** The only Saudi template that renders the section lists Arabic then English, and nothing else (F7). Jadarat's language list is a server lookup; its code treats English specially, as the only language for which a test score is validated (F3). The one Saudi software employer with a stated requirement screens English alone (F12, S22 F18). No source found lists a third language for this market.

4. **Which proficiency vocabulary?** Not one vocabulary; four are in play, and which one the applicant faces depends on the surface:
   - *A closed list the platform owns*: Jadarat's "مستوى الإتقان" (values unknown, F2) and SuccessFactors' three dropdowns per language (values per customer, F10).
   - *LinkedIn's five words*, Elementary, Limited working, Professional working, Full professional, Native or bilingual (F13), which is what arrives by import at Elm and by the LinkedIn URL Tamara and Lean ask for.
   - *A one-word ladder chosen by the applicant* in Saudi templates: اللغة الأم / طليق and Native / Fluent (F7), with مبتدئ / متوسط / متقدم and, for teachers, ممتاز / جيد جدًا / جيد offered as alternatives in the same platform's advice (F8). This is the only place ممتاز / جيد جدًا / جيد was found (F18).
   - *A test score*: IELTS, TOEFL and STEP are the three tests a Saudi employer names (S22 F18) and the two tests Jadarat once hard-coded (F3). IELTS bands are named and mapped to the CEFR only at the top (F15); STEP's own bands are unverified and Saudi institutions convert it three different ways (F16).
   The CEFR appears only in Sabbar's advice, glossed into Arabic, and never on a Saudi form or employer page (F8, F14). Speaking, reading and writing are rated separately only in SuccessFactors (F10); every other surface rates a language once.

5. **Whose choice is the vocabulary?** On Jadarat and SuccessFactors, the platform's or employer's (F2, F10). On LinkedIn, LinkedIn's (F13). On the resume document itself, the applicant's, and JSON Resume validates nothing (F19); the Saudi sources that model it use a single word per language (F7).

The strongest facts in this file are Jadarat's own code (F1 to F5), which is the national platform's form read directly; the weakest are the proficiency words themselves, since the two systems that own a closed list did not expose it, and the only Saudi vocabulary quoted is a platform blog.
